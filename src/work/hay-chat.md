## Product

I opened the Hay backlog on 27 September 2025. It included a full GDPR epic from the start, because the EU-hosted positioning depends on it: data subject requests, retention, redaction, data residency and a subprocessor registry among them. Almost all of it shipped between December 2025 and April 2026.

Bigger ideas went through a research spike and a go/no-go gate before any build. I also ran written QA passes with acceptance criteria on five integrations before they shipped. We planned releases in Jira until summer 2026, then moved to direct product reviews between the two of us.

## Website and search

The 31 August 2026 baseline showed the problem: most search traffic was brand confusion. I refocused the site on real buyer searches, with one page per search meaning, each checked on the live results page first. That meant integration pages, compare pages, a glossary, guides and a press section, plus the technical work behind them. I directed Claude Code as the build team through 32 scoped work packages, each with a written brief that I reviewed before it merged. By 22 October, 28-day Google impressions were up 19% to 9,056 and a key comparison post had moved from position 9.1 to 4.9.

## Pitching, outbound and events

For the Startup World Cup at Dublin Tech Summit I cut the pitch from 6:01 to 4:48 to fit a five-minute slot, and we made the Irish final. For SIM Conference in Portugal and Dublin Tech Summit I ranked the goals (press first, then customers, then partners) and left investment out. I wrote the offer cards to stay useful for follow-ups through Q3. At Web Summit Lisbon 2025 my co-founder and I screened about 300 contacts by hand, which gave us 67 qualified leads. Outbound ran as six segmented Lemlist campaigns on two new sending domains, warmed up before launch.
